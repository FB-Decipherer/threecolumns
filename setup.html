Setup

The sheet that produces a page. Nothing here is a login and nothing here is protected, because there is nothing behind it to protect — it is a form that writes files. Fill it in, copy the output, paste it into the file, publish.

D1Page header

Two kinds of page. A standing page says what it is under the label What this is, and carries no date. A running series puts the day’s claim under Today’s claim; its date then appears in four places, all four must agree, and this sheet writes all four from the one field.

What this is



D2The picture at the head of the page


Which picture heads the page, and where it sits: beside the title (the masthead picture, the usual place on a standing page) or under the header (a series issue’s front figure). Type the file names that are sitting in the folder, one per line; the chooser lists them and shows what you picked. Captions are set at 22px, larger than body text, because a caption is often the only thing a scanning reader reads.

















D3The two most recent content changes


The top of column B, the same on every site: Internal links, captioned The two most recent content changes., holding two cards, newest first — each a date and what changed, linked to the place on the page where it changed. One card reads as thin; three begins to look like a log. Leave the second set of fields empty on a page that has only ever changed once.



  

  









  

  












D4References


One card per external source. Order is fixed: kind, title, provenance, preview, why it is cited, then the link last. The link goes last on purpose — put it first and readers click it before reading the preview, which defeats the whole column.









D5One level up


The last thing on the page, below the footer: a card naming the site one level above this one, with one line saying what that site is, in its own words. It is threecolumns.online unless this site sits under another instance, as worland-dog-law.online sits under washakie-ai.online. That site is another website, so it also needs a card of its own in D4. Leave the address empty on a site with nothing above it and nothing is written.





  

  








D6Link health


A browser cannot test somebody else's link — a cross-origin request comes back with no status attached, so an in-page checker reports healthy links as dead. The check therefore runs on a schedule, away from the reader, and writes the small file below. Check anything known dead and give the date it was found.









If in doubt, leave it empty. A missing or empty links.json makes the page say nothing at all about link health. That is the correct default: no file means no claim, and never a false alarm.
