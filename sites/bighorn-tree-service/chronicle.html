What has been happening

A plain account of how this page has changed and why, newest first. It is one day old, so this is a short record — but a page whose entire argument is that a stranger should be able to check things had better start keeping one on the first day rather than the hundredth.

Where it stands

This is a sample, and says so across the masthead. Bighorn Basin Tree Service does not exist. It was built to show a Washakie County trade what a page of its own could look like.

It exists to solve one problem. A stranger standing in their own yard cannot tell whether the outfit quoting them is insured, and the usual proof is a folded certificate in a glovebox that nobody can check on a Saturday. So the insurance is the first answer on the page, and every line of it links to a check somebody else keeps.

The rules around it are real — Wyoming’s monopolistic workers’ compensation fund, OSHA’s ten feet from a power line, the arboricultural safety standard, the power company’s free pruning near its own lines. Thirteen sources, all fetched.

What has been happening

25 September 2026 — the page was too much to read, so it became two

Walter, looking at what had been built: “the page is way, way, way too much to read. It presupposes that everyone will be interested in reading all that background info.” He was right, and the numbers were blunt: column A alone had grown to 1,347 words across nine answers, on top of thirteen source cards and ten technical sections. Nothing had been deleted — the insurance block was intact and still first — but it had become one ninth of one column, which for the person it was built for is the same as losing it.

So the front page is now a card: the name, a telephone number big enough to hit with a thumb, one picture of the work being done, the insurance with its three checks, the service area, and a single quiet link to everything else. 197 words. The full three-column page is still there, unchanged, one tap away at details.html.

This is worth recording as a limit of the format rather than a failure of the page. Three columns are right for a subject with something to be checked at length — an ordinance audit, a grant proposal, a research strand. A tree service has a customer standing in a yard who wants four facts and a telephone number. The format had to learn to show a short face and keep the long one underneath.

24 September 2026 — the insurance block got a name

The block at the top of the page had no heading; it was simply a box under an answer. It is now labeled One-tap insurance verification, set in the same small lettering the masthead uses, so that what the page is offering is legible before a word of it is read. Walter’s wording, and better than what was there.

24 September 2026 — built, around one finding

The page exists because of something turned up while researching it, which is the right order for these things.

Wyoming runs a monopolistic workers’ compensation fund — the state is the insurer, not a private company — and there is no public lookup where you can type a business name and see whether it is covered. What there is instead is better: any third party can enter a contractor’s federal employer number and their own address, and the State of Wyoming emails them a Certificate of Good Standing covering workers’ compensation and unemployment insurance. It never passes through the contractor’s hands.

That is why this page prints its federal employer number, which trade sites never do. It invites checking, and inviting checking is the whole proposition.

The page also sends work away on purpose. A power line in a tree is the utility’s call, free, with their telephone number given — because OSHA’s rule is ten feet for anyone not qualified for line clearance, and a tree service that takes the job anyway is gambling with somebody else’s life on your property. Turning down the work is more persuasive than any claim on the page.

One deliberate departure from the format, recorded on the page itself: the first answer is open when the page loads. Everywhere else in this family, column A starts folded shut.

Still open

  • No real tree service has seen it. The insurance figures, the credential and the federal employer number are all sample values. A real company’s would be its own, and the links would go to the same three places.
  • The ISA credential lookup could not be read by machine, so the source card describes the credential from the society’s main site rather than from the register itself. The exam prerequisites and renewal period are deliberately left off this page rather than guessed at.
  • The license arrangement applies here too — the repository’s license currently reaches this page’s words. It is written up on the method site’s Page F, unfixed.
  • The split is new and untested. Nobody has yet arrived at the card cold and tried to decide whether to call. That is the only test that matters and it has not been run.
  • Which face a site shows should be a setting, not a rebuild. Walter’s suggestion is that the setup sheet — page D — carry it as a choice, so a subject picks short or long without anybody editing HTML. Not built.
