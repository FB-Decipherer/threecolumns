What has been happening

A plain account of how this page has changed and why, newest first. The farm is invented; the record of building it is not, and a sample that asks to be trusted about a county’s crops and water should be willing to show its working. Each entry says what changed and what it was for.

Where it stands

This is a sample, and says so across the masthead. East Bench Pivot Farm does not exist. It was built to show a real Washakie County farm what a page of its own could look like, before anybody has to pay for one.

Everything around the farm is real. The county, the crops, the water, the hay market, the rules about selling beef by the share and hay certified weed-free for the mountains — all of it sourced in the middle column, because a sample that invents its facts teaches nothing.

It has a job in a grant proposal. It is the thing to open when somebody asks what twelve websites for a county would actually be.

What has been happening

24 September 2026 — a second recent-change card, and the same opening line as the family

The middle column now carries two recent changes rather than one. On a farm page this matters more than it sounds: a single card is a headline, and what a returning customer actually wants is whether the third cutting is in and whether the fall beef is still open. Column C also gained the family’s standard opening sentence, so the page promises the same thing here that it promises everywhere else.

22 September 2026 — the format caught up underneath it

Nothing about the farm changed. What changed was the method it is built on: the argument about a team of one, the split between standing a site up and sending a change live, and a proper account of how somebody edits their own words later. A sample is only worth anything if it keeps pace with the thing it is a sample of.

21 September 2026 — built, stamped, and moved into place

Written in an afternoon: hay by the bale or the ton, beef by the quarter, sugar beets and malt barley under contract, and sixteen previewed sources behind them.

Then two decisions that mattered more than the writing. The SAMPLE stencil was painted across the masthead, and a dashed box added saying plainly that the farm is invented — because a convincing fake farm with real county data is exactly the kind of thing that ends up quoted as real. And the page was moved to live under threecolumns.online in a folder rather than buying a domain of its own, which is itself one of the arguments the proposal makes: a family of sites does not need a family of bills.

The same day it was linked from the grant proposal, and the idea was first written down that the county’s FFA chapters might carry pages like this one to the farms. Nobody has asked them.

Still open

  • No real farm has seen it. That is the whole test and it has not been run. Until a farmer says “that is not how hay is sold here,” the page is a guess made carefully.
  • The FFA ambassador idea rests partly on this page, and remains an idea. Nobody has approached either chapter.
  • The license arrangement applies here too. As things stand, the repository’s license reaches this page’s words. Harmless for an invented farm; it would not be harmless for a real one, and it is written up on the method site’s Page F.
